# Rabbit Hole Roasters — Company Background

> A fictional company, made up for Deck Edit's sample project. Any resemblance to real space
> agencies, coffee companies or Queens is entirely coincidental. The characters come from Lewis
> Carroll's *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (1865), which is in the public domain.

## Founding story

Alice founded Rabbit Hole Roasters after falling — once again — down a rabbit hole, which this time
let out in low Earth orbit, 410 km up, aboard a half-finished habitat module. The crew offered her
coffee from a foil pouch through a straw. She was not amused.

"If I had a world of my own," she said, "everything would be nonsense — and the coffee would stay
in the cup." The Mad Hatter, who had come along for the tea, pointed out that it was always six
o'clock somewhere and that the module had plenty of corners. The company was founded that
afternoon, which was also an unbirthday.

## Products

**The Drink Me Pod** is a two-part system:

1. **The brewer** spins a 5 cm brew chamber at 134 rpm, giving the grounds one *g* of artificial
   gravity, so hot water percolates the way it would on Earth.
2. **The cup** has no round bottom: its cross-section is a teardrop with a sharp interior corner.
   Surface tension pulls coffee along the corner toward the rim, so you can sip it like a normal
   cup — no straw, no pouch, no escaping latte. (Real-world inspiration: the capillary "space cup"
   experiments flown on the International Space Station.)

## Risks we take seriously

- **Regulation.** The Queen of Hearts chairs the Orbital Beverage Authority. Her standard ruling is
  "Off with their license!" We file early, and we make her the Red Velvet.
- **Spills.** A loose latte in orbit is a hazard. The capillary cup keeps the liquid pinned; the
  brewer is sealed until the cup docks.
- **Debris.** Jabberwocky-class debris is rare but bad. The café ring is shielded.
- **Time.** The White Rabbit's watch is exactly two days slow ("it was the *best* butter"). We use
  the station clock.

## Glossary

- **Bond number (Bo):** gravity vs. surface tension. On Earth, gravity wins in a cup; in orbit,
  surface tension wins, so the shape of the cup decides where the coffee goes.
- **Concus–Finn condition:** a liquid wicks into a corner when the corner half-angle plus the
  contact angle is under 90°. Our cup's corner is 15°, coffee on its coating is about 30° — wicks.
- **Unbirthday:** any day that isn't your birthday. Revenue-relevant.
